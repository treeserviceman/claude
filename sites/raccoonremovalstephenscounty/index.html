Is It Raccoons? Look For This

  • 1
    Torn soffits, vents, or roof edges

    Raccoons use their strength and dexterity to rip open weak points around rooflines and gable vents to get inside.

  • 2
    Trash cans knocked over or raided repeatedly

    Raccoons denning nearby will often forage close to home, and unsecured trash is an easy target.

  • 3
    Raccoons seen at dusk near the roofline

    Repeated sightings of a raccoon climbing your home in the same spot usually means it already has a way inside.

  • 4
    Paw prints in dust, snow, or mud near the roofline

    Distinct five-toed tracks around downspouts, decks, or roof access points are a strong indicator of raccoon traffic.

  • 5
    Chewed or displaced roof vent covers

    Raccoons will pry loose or chew through plastic and metal vent covers to widen an opening into the attic.

  • 6
    Damaged chimney caps or spark arrestors

    A bent, missing, or pried-open chimney cap is one of the most common raccoon access points in older homes.
