Common Warning Signs

  • 1
    Torn soffits, vents, or roof edges

    Raccoons use their strength and dexterity to rip open weak points around rooflines and gable vents to get inside.

  • 2
    A strong, musky odor

    Urine and droppings build up quickly in a den site and produce a distinct, unpleasant smell you can often notice from inside the home.

  • 3
    Unusual pet behavior near a wall or vent

    Dogs and cats will often bark, whine, or stare fixedly at a wall, ceiling, or vent where wildlife is denning nearby.

  • 4
    Raccoons seen at dusk near the roofline

    Repeated sightings of a raccoon climbing your home in the same spot usually means it already has a way inside.

  • 5
    Trash cans knocked over or raided repeatedly

    Raccoons denning nearby will often forage close to home, and unsecured trash is an easy target.

  • 6
    Rustling sounds after dark

    Raccoons are nocturnal, so most activity — and most noise — happens once the house has gone quiet for the night.

Fairfield County Raccoon Problems, Solved

Attics in Fairfield County make ideal raccoon dens: dark, insulated, elevated, and usually quiet during the day. Once one gets into an attic, it rarely stays alone for long — mothers den up with litters, and what starts as a scratching noise at night can turn into torn insulation, chewed wiring, and a lingering odor problem within weeks. For Fairfield County homeowners and businesses, that means one team handling inspection through final repair, start to finish.

Homes and businesses across Fairfield County deal with seasonal weather swings that push wildlife to seek shelter indoors — exactly the conditions that send raccoons looking for a warm, dry attic to den in.
